# Hampshire & Hampden Canal: History & Route Map

The **Hampshire & Hampden Canal** was the Massachusetts continuation of Connecticut's [Farmington Canal](/farmington-canal). It connected the state-line area at Southwick with Northampton, forming the northern section of the New Haven and Northampton Canal system. Historical references also use **Northampton Canal**; the [American Canal Society's canal index](https://americancanalsociety.org/wp-content/uploads/2019/08/New-Haven-Northampton-Canal3-1.pdf) records these related names.

Use the [interactive Hampshire & Hampden Canal map](#canal-map) to follow the reconstructed waterway through western Massachusetts. The map opens on this section, with canal lines, locks, feeders, and Lidar terrain for comparison.

## The route from Southwick to Northampton

Follow the canal north through [Southwick](/towns/southwick), [Westfield](/towns/westfield), [Southampton](/towns/southampton), [Easthampton](/towns/easthampton), and [Northampton](/towns/northampton).

Each town presents a different part of the engineering story. The Southwick history describes navigation through the Congamond Ponds area. Westfield's account follows the river crossings and feeder canals. Southampton and Easthampton describe locks, embankments, and the Manhan River crossings, before the route reaches its northern terminus in Northampton.

## A canal completed in stages

| Date | Milestone |
| --- | --- |
| 1829 | The connection through Southwick to Westfield was open. |
| 1835 | The canal reached Northampton, completing the through connection to New Haven. |
| Late 1840s | Canal transport gave way to the railroad. |

The [Massachusetts historical landscape account of Southwick](https://www.mass.gov/files/documents/2016/08/wq/southwick.pdf) describes the staged opening, maintenance difficulties, and eventual railroad conversion. Historic Northampton's [description of Carl Walter's canal map](https://www.historic-northampton.org/products/guides/hampshirecanal.html) places the through connection between New Haven and Northampton in the 1835–1847 period. Local histories may discuss activity outside those dates; they describe individual sections as well as the full route.

## Places to examine on the map

**Southwick and the Congamond Ponds.** Start at the southern end of the Massachusetts section and compare the mapped canal with the ponds. Read about the floating towpath and summit water supply in the [Southwick canal history](/towns/southwick).

**Westfield's river crossings.** Follow the canal through the valley, then trace the Little River and Westfield River feeders away from the main line. The [Westfield history](/towns/westfield) explains how aqueducts, dams, and repairs shaped operations. Massachusetts also documents the later canal-and-railroad landscape in its [Westfield heritage report](https://www.mass.gov/files/documents/2016/08/my/westfield.pdf).

**The approach to Northampton.** Compare the route through [Southampton](/towns/southampton) and [Easthampton](/towns/easthampton) with the mapped locks and elevation profile. Continue into [Northampton](/towns/northampton) to explore the terminus and the canal's connection with local trade.

## Finding canal remains with Lidar

An embankment or cut can remain visible in terrain imagery even where vegetation makes it difficult to recognize from above. Other sections have disappeared under later development. The line shown here includes historical reconstruction, so a mapped feature is not necessarily a surviving or publicly accessible structure.

The Massachusetts reconstruction draws on Carl Walter's maps alongside terrain evidence and other historical sources. The [mapmaking account](/about) explains the comparisons and limitations. For deeper research, consult the [2023 archaeological reconnaissance survey](https://docs.farmingtoncanal.info/HHC_SWCA_PVPC_Cultural_Resources_2023-06.pdf) and the [complete source list](/sources).

To follow the waterway south of Massachusetts, visit the [Farmington Canal guide](/farmington-canal). To compare both state sections at once, return to the [full New Haven and Northampton Canal map](/).
